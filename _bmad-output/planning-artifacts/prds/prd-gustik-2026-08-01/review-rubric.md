# PRD Quality Review — Gustik (prd-gustik-2026-08-01)

## Overall verdict

This is a well-calibrated hobby-stakes PRD: honest about its assumptions (5 inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags, all indexed, no drift), light on ceremony where the shape calls for it, and — notably — it doesn't hide from the fact that wind data feeds a real capsizing-risk judgment call (§12 is a dedicated safety-framing section, not an afterthought). The one substantive hole is that the connectivity-loss safety story told in UJ-1 (§2.3) isn't actually backed by an FR that works for every persona who consumes the dashboard: the LED (FR-5) only helps someone standing next to the physical unit, but Klára (UJ-2) and any other remote dashboard viewer have no way to know displayed data might be stale. Everything else — glossary discipline, ID continuity, scope honesty, shape fit — is solid execution of what this rubric asks a hobby/solo PRD to do.

## Decision-readiness — adequate

Most of the document does real decision work: FR-9's public-link-vs-password call is stated as a decision with a rationale, not smoothed into "considerations"; FR-4's buffer is explicitly "best-effort, ne garance" with a named counter-metric (SM-C2); §12 explicitly accepts the risk of *not* building automated wind-threshold alerts and states why ("riziko falešného pocitu bezpečí... je akceptované"). The two `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts (§6.2 tilt compensation, §12 UX copy about data being orientational) sit at genuine tensions, not safe checkpoints. Open Questions (§9) are genuinely open — each ties to a concrete unresolved variable (Wi-Fi range, magnetometer placement, switch necessity), not rhetorical questions answered in the next clause.

The gap: UJ-1 (§2.3) tells a specific safety story — "pokud stanice právě ztratila spojení (signalizováno LED na jednotce), Tomáš ví, že zobrazené číslo může být zastaralé, a rozhoduje o to opatrněji" — but the mechanism it leans on (FR-5) is physically local: an LED on the Stanice enclosure. That works for Tomáš only if he's positioned to see the physical unit. It does not work at all for Klára (UJ-2), who by her own scenario is "na startu" viewing the same dashboard on her phone, with no line of sight to the boat-mounted LED. Nothing in FR-7 (current value), FR-8 (graph), or FR-9 (public access) gives the Dashboard itself any staleness indicator (e.g., "last updated Xs ago," a visual flag when the feed is stale). So the PRD's own edge case — the one place it explicitly reasons about a safety-relevant failure mode — quietly assumes an audience (people next to the boat) narrower than the audience it defines a page earlier (§2.1: referees *and* troop leaders, explicitly "stejná živá data"). This is a dodged trade-off, not a surfaced one: the PRD doesn't say "remote viewers won't know about staleness, and that's accepted because X."

### Findings
- **high** Dashboard has no staleness signal for remote viewers (§2.3 UJ-1/UJ-2, FR-5, FR-7, FR-9) — FR-5's connectivity-loss LED is on-device only; UJ-1's safety reasoning ("dashboard shows current value, Tomáš knows to be more careful if disconnected") depends on seeing that LED, which UJ-2's Klára — an explicitly in-scope dashboard user per FR-9 — structurally cannot do. No FR requires the Dashboard to surface a last-updated timestamp or stale-data indicator. *Fix:* add an FR (or a "Důsledek" under FR-7/FR-9) requiring the Dashboard to show when data was last received, or visually flag the current-value tile when the feed is stale — this is a small addition with outsized safety value given the stated stakes.

## Substance over theater — strong

Four named UJ protagonists (Tomáš ×2, Klára, Petra), each tied to a distinct JTBD from §2.1 — no filler personas, no persona used only for atmosphere. No differentiation/competitive section was force-fitted in (appropriate — this isn't a market play). §11's cross-cutting constraints name specific parts (ESP32-WROOM, HMC5883L, its lack of tilt compensation, I2C bus-length trade-off) rather than reciting "must be scalable/secure/reliable" boilerplate. The Vision (§1) is concrete to this project — DIY-build-it-yourself as a co-equal goal, salvaged WH1080/WH1090 sensors, yaw correction via magnetometer — and would not drop cleanly into another PRD.

## Strategic coherence — strong

The PRD names two theses and is honest that they're both real: safety-decision support (§1, first paragraph) and "build it myself as an end in itself, not just a means" (§1, second paragraph: "Stejně důležitý je druhý cíl projektu"). Feature prioritization follows from the safety thesis, not from ease: the explicit priority ladder in §6.2 ("funkční živé čtení > historický graf > vyladěná mechanika") matches what UJ-1/UJ-2 actually need first. Success metrics are mostly operational/deployment-shaped (SM-1, SM-2, SM-6) rather than vanity activity metrics, appropriate for a single-station hobby tool, and two counter-metrics (SM-C1, SM-C2) explicitly offset the risk of over-polishing accuracy or data-loss guarantees at the expense of shipping by 17. 8.

### Findings
- **medium** Dual-thesis arbitration left implicit (§1) — the PRD names "build it myself" as a goal on par with the safety thesis, but never states what happens if they conflict — e.g., if the DIY sensor path risks the 17. 8. deadline, does "functional over perfect" (§1) permit substituting a bought component? The PRD's own risk framing (§11.1: WH1080/WH1090 reuse is "nízkoriziková, vyšlapaná cesta") suggests this conflict is judged unlikely to bite, but that judgment is never stated — a reader can't tell whether it was considered or just didn't come up. *Fix:* one sentence in §1 or §8: "if a DIY component threatens the deadline, buy/substitute rather than slip" (or the opposite, if that's the actual intent).

## Done-ness clarity — adequate

Every FR carries a "Důsledky (testovatelné)" block, and most give a real bound: FR-4's 4-hour buffer target, FR-11's ≥8h runtime, FR-2's 8-cardinal-direction resolution, FR-13's explicit list of what "no field interaction" excludes. Where a number isn't nailed down, it's usually tagged `[ASSUMPTION]` and indexed rather than left as unmarked hand-waving (FR-1 sampling interval, FR-4 buffer size). That discipline is the strongest part of this dimension.

Two spots still read as adjective-graded rather than bounded, which matters more here than in a typical hobby PRD because both sit on the safety-relevant path:

### Findings
- **medium** FR-5 connectivity-loss signal has no time bound (§4.2, FR-5) — "Signalizace se aktivuje do několika vteřin od zjištění výpadku" ("within a few seconds") is the "reasonable performance"-style phrase the rubric calls out explicitly. Given this is the mechanism the safety story in UJ-1 depends on, an engineer implementing it has no acceptance threshold to build or test against. *Fix:* pick a number (even a soft one, `[ASSUMPTION]`-tagged) — e.g. "≤10s."
- **low** FR-12 enclosure durability has no bound (§4.5, FR-12) — "odolá stříkající vodě a dešti během typického provozu" has no IP-rating-equivalent or test condition (e.g., "survives N minutes of light rain," "IPX4-equivalent"). Reasonable to leave loose for a hobby build, but as written there's no way to know if the enclosure "passed."
- **low** FR-11 doesn't state recovery behavior after unplanned power loss (§4.4, FR-11) — the FR covers the happy-path 8h runtime but not what happens if the powerbank dies mid-day and is swapped/recharged (does the Stanice resume unattended?). This is very likely already covered by FR-13's boot-time auto-connect, but FR-11 itself doesn't say so, leaving it to inference.

## Scope honesty — strong

§5 (Nezahrnuto) does real work — 8 explicit exclusions, each with a one-line reason, not a token list. Every inline `[ASSUMPTION]` (5 total: FR-1, FR-2, FR-4, FR-13, §6.2) is indexed in §10 with matching content, and there are no orphaned index entries — clean roundtrip. The two `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts flag genuinely deferred decisions rather than padding. Open-items density (6 Open Questions + 5 assumptions + 2 NOTE FOR PM = 13, against 14 FRs) is high in absolute terms but appropriate per the rubric's own calibration — this is a hobby/solo PRD 16 days from a hard deadline, and the openness is honest rather than a stand-in for missing analysis.

## Downstream usability — strong

Glossary (§3) is genuinely used: `Stanice`, `Backend`, `Dashboard`, `Lokální buffer`, `Výpadek spojení`, `RSSI`, `Zprovoznění`, `Rozjezd` all recur with consistent meaning across FRs and UJs. ID sequences are contiguous with no gaps or duplicates — FR-1…FR-14, UJ-1…UJ-4, SM-1…SM-7 plus SM-C1/C2. Cross-references resolve (e.g., FR-3 → "viz FR-6," FR-12 → "viz Otevřené otázky a §11.1" both check out). All four UJs carry a named, distinct protagonist with inline context (Tomáš, Klára, Petra) — no floating UJs. This PRD explicitly feeds `bmad-architecture` next (per addendum.md's opening line), so this dimension matters more than for a standalone doc, and it's in good shape for that handoff.

## Shape fit — strong

This is close to the textbook example of a correctly calibrated hobby/solo PRD per the rubric's own guidance: light ceremony (no market sizing, no competitive analysis, no elaborate NFR matrix) paired with a substance bar that holds where it counts. §12 ("Omezení a mantinely — bezpečnost") is the clearest evidence the PRD didn't wave away the safety stakes just because the project is a hobby build — it states plainly that Gustik is decision *support*, not an automated safety system, and flags the false-safety risk of skipping alerts. UJ density (4, each with a distinct protagonist and JTBD) is right-sized for a tool with genuinely different consumer roles (referee vs. troop leader vs. builder) rather than over-formalized for a single-operator tool.
